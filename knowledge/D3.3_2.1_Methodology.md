The design methodology of HYPER-AI architecture is guided by the principles of modularity, scalability and self-management. The overarching aim is to enable the seamless execution of data-intensive applications across the full spectrum of the computing continuum spanning from resource-constrained IoT devices at the edge to high-performance cloud infrastructures.

This section outlines the high-level design principles and the architectural approach adopted to align with the project's vision of an intelligent, hyper-distributed system.

HYPER-AI follows a layered, service-oriented architecture that abstracts resource heterogeneity and promotes interoperability through standardized interfaces and horizontal resource abstractions. Key architectural principles include:

Kubernetes-Native Extension: Kubernetes (K8s)3 is adopted as the core orchestration substrate, extended through Custom Resource Definitions (CRDs), DaemonSets, and dedicated connectors to manage non-container-native devices such as Android Head Mounted Displays (HMDs) and constrained IoT nodes.

Computational Resources Abstraction: Resources across all layers (IoT, edge, cloud) are abstracted using the HYPER-AI Resource Model (HRM) to enable uniform access and orchestration, regardless of their native capabilities or interfaces.

Self-CHOP Autonomy: The architecture embeds self-CHOP capabilities, enabling dynamic, autonomous system behavior with minimal human intervention.

DLT-Based Trust and Security: HYPER-AI incorporates distributed ledger technologies (DLTs) to enforce decentralized, zero-trust security models. These include decentralized identity management, distributed access control, audit trails, and tamper-proof data exchange across the computing continuum.

The architectural framework is explicitly aligned with the conceptual model of the computing continuum, which spans multiple layers of computing from on-device and on-premise deployments to near-edge, far-edge, and centralized cloud infrastructures. At the heart of the HYPER-AI computing continuum landscape lies the seamless integration of diverse computational environments including:

IoT Devices and On-Device Nodes: Devices such as sensors, AR glasses, and microcontrollers are integrated via lightweight communication protocols and connectors that translate their capabilities into Kubernetes-compatible descriptors.

Edge Infrastructure: Near and far-edge nodes are equipped with enhanced awareness and resource monitoring capabilities, allowing them to participate in autonomous cluster formation and workload execution.

Cloud Infrastructure: High-performance centralized resources provide additional computing power and storage capacity for tasks requiring global state, deep analytics, or large-scale inference.
